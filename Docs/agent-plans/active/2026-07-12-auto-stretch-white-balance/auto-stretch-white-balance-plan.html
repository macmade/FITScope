Managed Auto-Stretch & White-Balance Exclusion

Generated — 2026-07-12
Source — design discussion in this session (no source document on disk)

Since per-channel auto-STF landed (2026-07-11-per-channel-auto-stf), the unlinked Screen Transfer neutralizes the background colour cast on its own — it is now a second background neutralizer alongside white balance. The two do not compose: white balance runs before the stretch in the pipeline (Normalize → WhiteBalance → BrightnessContrast → Stretch), but the per-channel STF's midtones are fit to the raw, pre-white-balance channel medians. Turning white balance on shifts those medians, so the STF no longer lands each background on the target and the cast returns. Re-running Auto cannot fix it: the derivation reads only the immutable source (detectionImage / autoStretchColorSource), so it is invariant to white balance and yields the identical STF.

The chosen resolution treats per-channel STF and white balance as mutually-exclusive background neutralizers. The one-shot Auto button becomes a persistent Auto (managed) toggle. While managed, a uniform STF composes cleanly with white balance (a single curve applied to all channels preserves whatever balance WB set, for any gains, with no re-fit needed), while a per-channel STF excludes white balance. When an action would make both active at once, a last-action-wins rule resolves it: the control you just touched stays, the other yields — silently when nothing is lost, behind a confirmation when white balance would be removed. Hand-editing the stretch drops fully to manual mode, where per-channel STF and white balance may coexist freely with no forcing. This is a behaviour + UI + state change; it does not alter any stretch or white-balance maths, and it leaves white balance in its correct linear, pre-stretch pipeline slot.

Questions & answers

Is applying white balance before the STF correct?

Yes. White balance is a linear per-channel gain (colour calibration); it is only meaningful on the linear, scene-referred signal, so its pre-stretch slot is right. The fix is not to reorder the pipeline. (Brightness/contrast being pre-stretch is a separate, parked question — out of scope here.)

Which direction resolves the WB ↔ STF collision?

Direction B: a uniform STF composes with white balance, a per-channel STF does not. So when WB is doing the balancing the STF should be uniform; when the per-channel STF is doing the balancing, WB is redundant. Treat them as mutually exclusive rather than stacking two neutralizers.

Should the one-click Auto become a toggle?

Yes. Auto becomes a persistent managed mode. While engaged the STF is app-derived and its linking is governed by the mutual-exclusion rule; the moment the user hand-edits the stretch, Auto disengages.

If I add white balance while a per-channel auto STF is on, what happens?

The STF yields: it switches to uniform, silently (an auto STF is recomputable, nothing is lost), and white balance stays on. The image stays balanced across the toggle.

If I engage per-channel auto while white balance is on, should it disable WB?

Yes — the symmetric dual of the rule above. Per-channel is the newer intent, so white balance yields. Because removing WB reverses something the user explicitly enabled, it goes through a confirmation rather than happening silently.

Does the exclusion apply in manual mode too?

No. The forcing/exclusion is a behaviour of the Auto (managed) toggle only. In manual mode a hand-built per-channel STF and white balance may coexist freely, with no forcing and no confirm (at most a passive hint).

(a) Does hand-editing a per-channel STF drop fully to manual?

Yes. A hand-edited stretch is manual, so it is never force-switched. This makes a switch-to-uniform only ever happen to an auto STF (no edits to lose), so that direction is always silent — the only confirmation in the whole system is white-balance removal.

(b) Confirm on all white-balance removal, or only manual gains?

All. Confirm whenever an action would remove white balance, whether it is Auto (gray-world) or Manual gains — for predictability, even though auto WB carries no data to lose.

What should the confirmation offer?

Three choices: Cancel (back out entirely — per-channel not applied, WB stays, nothing changes), Keep White Balance (apply per-channel and keep WB — they coexist, dropping to manual mode), and Remove White Balance (default — apply per-channel, WB off, the clean exclusive result).

Should the app silently change a control in another section?

Only when nothing is lost, and always visibly: when one control disables the other, show an inline note (e.g. the WB section reading “Off — per-channel stretch is handling colour balance”) so the cross-section change is explained rather than spooky.

What does turning the Auto toggle off do?

It freezes the current STF as a manual value — reversible, since re-enabling Auto re-derives. It does not clear the stretch and does not touch white balance.

Notes

  • The one unifying rule. Whenever an action would make a per-channel STF and white balance both active while Auto is engaged, the control just touched stays and the other yields. STF yields → uniform (silent). WB yields → off (confirm). Everything else in this plan is a consequence of that rule plus “hand-edit ⇒ manual”.
  • Derivation is invariant to adjustments. ImageRenderer.autoScreenTransferSettings → ImageRenderSource.autoStretchSettings → ImageProcessor.autoStretchSettings read only the immutable source (detectionImage / autoStretchColorSource), never the live pipeline. Uniform-vs-per-channel is the linking choice passed in; both are derived from the same raw source, so switching linking is a cheap re-derive, not a processed-buffer pass. This is why uniform + WB needs no per-tick recompute.
  • New state, scoped to the stretch. One flag — “Auto engaged” — on ImageAdjustments, cleared on any manual stretch edit. The existing baseline/opened provenance model and hasAdjustments stay as they are; an image that opened auto-stretched starts with Auto engaged so scenario 1 (add WB → uniform) works out of the box.
  • Model exposes intent; the view confirms. ImageAdjustments is a @MainActor model and must not present UI. The collision resolution that needs a confirmation (WB removal) is surfaced as an explicit request the control layer presents before committing; the silent resolutions (STF → uniform) are applied directly in the model.
  • Key files. Model: FITScope/Model/ImageAdjustments.swift, FITScope/Model/ImageRenderer.swift, FITScope/Model/ImageRenderSource.swift. UI: FITScope/Views/ImageControls/StretchControlView.swift, FITScope/Views/ImageControls/WhiteBalanceControlView.swift, FITScope/Views/ScreenTransfer/STFEditorView.swift, FITScope/Views/Inspector/InspectorView.swift. Loaders' openedSettings(…) seed the on-open engaged state.
  • Auto toggled OFF (confirmed). Turning Auto off freezes the current STF as a manual value (reversible; re-enabling Auto re-derives). It does not clear the stretch or touch white balance.
  • Out of scope. The brightness/contrast pre-stretch ordering question is parked and not addressed here. No stretch or white-balance maths changes. No SwiftPixel submodule changes are expected; the derivation already supports both linkings.
  • Xcode + package kept in sync. New files (if any) must be added to both the .xcodeproj and any package manifest, and tests run through xcodebuild.

2

Mutual-exclusion resolution (last-action-wins)

Completed
Goal

Encode the core rule in the model: while Auto is engaged, a per-channel STF and white balance cannot both be active. Resolve collisions by last-action-wins, distinguishing the silent path (STF → uniform) from the path that needs a confirmation (WB removal), which is surfaced as an explicit request for the view to present.

Tasks
  • Adding/enabling white balance while a managed per-channel STF is active re-derives the STF as uniform and applies it silently (WB stays on).
  • Requesting a per-channel managed STF while white balance is active does not commit silently; it produces a resolution request the view resolves (see Milestone 4).
  • Expose the resolution outcomes the view can commit: apply per-channel + remove WB, apply per-channel + keep WB (drop to manual), and cancel.
  • Confine the exclusion to managed mode: in manual mode no forcing occurs and both may coexist.
  • Guarantee no collision state is ever committed while managed (invariant), and that switching linking is a re-derive off the main actor, consistent with the existing autoScreenTransferSettings offload.
  • Unit-test each transition of the truth table (see Appendix), asserting the resulting stretch linking, whiteBalance, and isAutoStretch.

Implementation notes
  • Approach. TDD — the truth-table tests were written first (all rows of the Appendix), then the model API implemented until green. Full ImageAdjustmentsTests: 28/28 passing via xcodebuild.
  • Design (confirmed with the user before implementing). The rule lives on ImageAdjustments (Option A). Because the model holds no render source, it reaches the derivation through an injected closure deriveAutoStretch: ( _ uniform: Bool ) async -> ImageProcessor.Settings? (defaults to a no-op), wired by ImageRenderer.init with [ weak self ] to autoScreenTransferSettings( linking: uniform ? .uniform : .automatic ). This keeps every control talking only to adjustments and makes the whole truth table unit-testable with a stubbed closure — no fake render source. A plain Bool parameter avoids coupling the model to the renderer's ScreenTransferLinking enum.
  • Managed-set seam (deferred from Milestone 1). A private isApplyingManagedStretch guard lets the managed re-derivation assign stretch (and its normalization) without the didSet disengaging isAutoStretch. applyManagedStretch(_:) applies { normalize, stretch } together (matching the one-shot Auto path) and returns Bool so callers commit dependent state only when a stretch actually applied.
  • Rule methods (Tasks 1–5). setWhiteBalance(_:) async — enabling WB while managed + per-channel silently re-derives uniform and keeps WB; every other case just sets the mode. requestPerChannelAutoStretch() async — derives per-channel; applies + engages directly when WB is off (returns nil); returns a PerChannelStretchRequest (commits nothing) when WB is on. resolve(_:as:) commits remove WB (apply, WB off, stay managed) or keep WB (apply as a direct write ⇒ drop to manual, WB kept); cancel is simply not resolving. The exclusion is confined to managed mode; the invariant (per-channel STF and WB never both committed while managed) holds at every commit site.
  • Milestone boundary (intentional). Model-only: no view calls these methods yet, so the rule is dormant in the running app (the WB / stretch controls still write the properties directly). Milestones 3–5 wire the toggle, the confirmation, and the STF editor to route through this API.

Round 1 raised two Medium findings (both fixed, see below) plus Low/Info notes; round 2 (independent re-review of the fixed state) confirmed both fixes genuinely closed, traced the invariant under reentrant interleavings and found no violation, and reported no Critical/High/Medium issues — converged.
